How Strikethrough checks a deal

Last updated 29 November 2026

Strikethrough checks advertised Australian deals and tells you whether each one is genuinely good. Every deal gets a verdict and a score out of 100. This page explains exactly how, so you can judge the verdicts for yourself.

Where the prices come from

  • Retailer websites and catalogues (for example JB Hi-Fi, The Good Guys, Harvey Norman, Big W, Kmart, Kogan, Amazon AU and brand stores).
  • Dated deal reports from publications such as TechRadar and Tom's Guide.
  • OzBargain, where Australian shoppers post and vote on deals.
  • Meta's Ad Library, to see which retailers are advertising their sales.

Prices are checked twice a day during major sales, and each deal shows the date it was last checked.

The checks

  • Other stores. When more than one store sells the same product, we compare them. A price more than 2% above the cheapest store we track is marked Lower elsewhere.
  • Price history. Where we or a price tracker have recorded past prices, we look for record lows, prices close to a record low, prices a product often hits, and prices that were lower recently.
  • The was-price. If one store's was-price is more than 10% above another store's for the same product, we measure the discount from the lower one. That's the real discount. Very deep discounts with no history, and discounts on a store's own brand, are marked Was-price unconfirmed until we can check them.
  • Community votes. Popular OzBargain deals get a badge and a small score boost, but votes never override a price catch.

What each verdict means

  • Record low: the lowest price we or a tracker have recorded.
  • Near record low: within a few percent of the lowest recorded price.
  • Best price tracked: the cheapest (or equal cheapest) of the stores we track for this product.
  • Often this price: a real drop, but one the product regularly hits.
  • Not yet verified: the maths is right against the quoted was-price, but we haven't found price history or a second store to confirm it. It's not a judgment on the retailer.
  • Lower elsewhere: another store we track has it for more than 2% less.
  • Was cheaper recently: the product has sold for less earlier in the season.
  • Was-price unconfirmed: we couldn't confirm the was-price the discount is measured from.
  • Small discount: the saving is only a few percent.

The score

Each deal gets a score out of 100. Most of it comes from the verdict, then up to 28 points for how deep the real discount is, plus up to 5 for community votes. "Best deals first" sorts by this score.

Limits

We check a sample of widely promoted deals, not every product in every store. Prices change through the day and can depend on colour, size, membership or location. A verdict compares prices; it isn't a claim that any retailer has broken the law. Always confirm the price at checkout. If you think a verdict is wrong, please tell us.

Independence

Strikethrough is independent and run by Cameron Gibbens. Retailers don't pay for verdicts, scores or placement. Some links are affiliate links; commissions never change a verdict or a score.
